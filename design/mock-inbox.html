Four inboxes

Four directions for /runs, drawn with the real contents of the inbox on Friday 4 September. Two things run through all four, because they are wrong in the screen as it stands: a source is identifiable at a glance, and a row says enough to decide without opening it.

The two fixes underneath all four

Today a row says only mail or github in 11 px grey, then a title. So "Google: Security alert" and "homelab#602" are the same shape, and a mail tells you nothing about who sent it or what it says. Shape carries the source — colour is already spoken for by the state chips and the agent marks, per theme.css — and each source brings the two or three facts that actually decide it.

mail Sender name and address, subject, first line of the body. Plus: thread count, whether it is filed, which folder.
github repo#number, title, who opened it. Plus: checks passing or failing, diff size, whether review was requested of you.
schedule Outcome and the first sentence of what it said. Plus: how long it ran, what it cost, how many firings in a row ended this way.
bench Project and session, and what it is waiting on. Plus: agent, how long it has been idle.
memory The proposed sentence itself, not "a proposed memory". Plus: the session it was learned in.
calendar Time and title. Plus: where, who else, whether there is a link to join.
01

Dense triage list

Two lines per item instead of a card: mark, who, subject, snippet — then the facts for that source in mono underneath. Actions appear on the selected row rather than costing every row a button bar, and the keyboard walks it.

gains ~11 items per screen with more information each, from 6 with less · costs a busy row; the facts line has to be disciplined or it becomes noise

02

Two-pane reader

The list keeps the mark and the sender; the detail gets the width that is currently empty margin, and shows the whole thing — the mail body, the PR's checks, the session waiting on you — without leaving the list or losing your place in it. On a phone this is the same list, and tapping pushes the detail as its own screen.

gains the most information of the four, and a real reading surface · costs a second layout to maintain

03

Grouped by what it wants from you

Stop sorting by arrival. Sort by the kind of attention required, because that is the decision you are actually making: something to answer, something to review, something to read, something merely blocked. The mark still says which source, so a group stays legible as a mix. Open Loops folds into the group it belongs to instead of sitting above everything.

gains a screen that says what it is asking of you · costs the newest-first reading everyone knows, and needs triage to classify well

04

Bundled digest

The list is long because the same thing arrives repeatedly. Fold repeats and bulk sources into one row carrying its mark, a count and a bulk action; spend the saved room on the items that are genuinely singular, which get the full sender-and-snippet treatment. Twenty-one items becomes eight lines, and the four dependabot PRs clear with one press rather than eight.

gains the shortest list, bulk actions, and room for real detail on what is left · costs a bundling rule that will occasionally fold away something you wanted
